My Letterboxd in Numbers.

Personal Project Python · scikit-learn · Chart.js Updated as of April 12, 2026

Two years of logging every film I watch on Letterboxd produced enough data to do something interesting. A Python pipeline pulls my exports, analyzes rating patterns, genres, directors, and release years, then trains two ML models on my own reviews: one on review text, one on film properties. The question: can my writing, or the film itself, predict how much I liked it?

Python scikit-learn VADER Sentiment TF-IDF Ridge Regression TMDB API Chart.js Letterboxd

The data covers rated films and written reviews. Genre, director, actor, and financial metadata was pulled from the TMDB API. Two ML approaches are compared: one trained purely on review text using TF-IDF + VADER, and one trained on structured film properties like genre, director, runtime, budget, and TMDB crowd ratings. All analysis runs locally and only the output JSON files are served here.

Can My Words Predict My Rating?

Two models trained on written reviews using an 80/20 train/test split: a Ridge Regression predicting the exact star rating, and a 3-class Logistic Regression classifying each review as Meh (≤2.5★), Solid (3.0-3.5★), or Great (≥4.0★). Features are TF-IDF text vectors combined with VADER sentiment scores. Ridge was chosen over standard linear regression to avoid overfitting in the high-dimensional feature space. All metrics are from the held-out test set.

What It All Means

After two years of data and two rounds of modeling, a few things became clear about how I watch films and what actually drives my ratings.

Rating Patterns
I rate older films higher. Runtime helps too.

Films from earlier decades rate above average, part genuine quality, part survivorship bias. The longer the runtime, the higher the rating, suggesting I reward ambition and immersion over efficiency.

NLP Model
My words predict my ratings, but barely.

The text model beats the majority class baseline, but the gap is modest. VADER sentiment correlates only weakly with my star ratings, because I write analytically and with humor regardless of how much I liked a film.

Structured Model
Crowd consensus does nearly all the work.

With TMDB vote average included, the structured model performs reasonably well. Remove it and performance collapses to near zero. Genre, director, runtime, and cast alone explain almost nothing about my personal ratings.

The Honest Answer
My taste is less idiosyncratic than I thought.

The crowd tends to be right about what I'll like. The things that make me feel like a discerning viewer, the director, the genre, the runtime, matter far less than whether the film is broadly well-regarded. I'm a consensus viewer who writes like a critic.

Bottom Line

The best predictor of how I'll rate a film is how everyone else already rated it. The best predictor of how I'll write about it is the rating I gave. Two years of data to confirm I am, fundamentally, a person who agrees with Letterboxd.
